# Supported claims

The [explicit-table model](spec/table-relations.md) proves complete Boolean
laws for finite masked membership and exact indexed reads, including duplicate
and empty cases. Exact body equations and `FormulaLaw` proofs connect the
retained library formulas to these predicates under explicit reached length
bounds, preserving exhaustion elsewhere. The [model specification](spec/table-relations.md)
records this source-model boundary separately from native VM correspondence and
cryptographic lookup arguments.

The [selected property adapters](spec/selected-property-adapters.md) add a pinned
KZG evaluation-binding reduction with exact concrete opening-game equality and
explicit admissibility/budget closure, a separate two-read finite oracle-recovery
consumer of ArkLib Knowledge, and a private-producer concrete-verifier WP route
for canonical honest completeness. The latter is exercised on the actual
randomized Schnorr subject. KZG hardness is scoped to admitted algorithms; the
upstream all-functions assumption is not used. The finite recovery claim is
about an immutable two-cell sum relation, without KZG knowledge, native bytes,
or a compiler-stage receipt. The WP consumer's verifier must have a returning
deterministic table run; randomized validator continuations remain outside it.

The actual plain `CheckMembership` construction now has checked original and
artifact admission, exact aggregate preparation, one full nominal Process
binding and runtime equations preserving exhaustion and oracle/proof state.
`TableMembership.membership_completeness` proves successful actual paired
execution for mathematical selected membership under explicit reached length
bounds; `membership_false_rejected` proves deterministic rejection of false
mathematical statements under every permitted reply, even outside those bounds.
Nonvacuity has both empty-query and nonempty selected-row witnesses.
`ZkcSecurity.Examples.TableMembership.Claims` extends this same actual pair to
canonical compiled-ideal perfect completeness and adaptive zero-error soundness
receipts, including the empty charged quantity. The generated nominal ABI keeps
inactive values in its semantic public binding and appends no transcript to
plain runtime inputs. Both properties reuse the same whole-validator equation
and actual helper laws; the soundness game includes every raw typed statement.
`BatchOpening` separately admits and binds an actual aggregate send/receive
client, preserves its returned batch and refuses wrong nominal or empty-sequence
sorts. The [selected byte connection](spec/data-codecs.md) also relates its actual
constructed processes to the finite interpreted codec, preserving false answers,
stop behavior and unread frames. This is distinct from native ZKCV framing and
KZG security; baseline acceptance requires separate model review and replay.

All results below concern stated mathematical models and exact selected imports.
They do not certify arbitrary DSL programs or the full native lowering pipeline.

The [component installation](spec/components.md) also checks a sequential
Schnorr/Chaum–Pedersen producer with private draws interleaved with public
challenges, through generated whole-process views and a canonical compiled-ideal
completeness receipt. Reusable sampled readback composes over actual returned
values, service state and transcript histories. This adds honest completeness
coverage, under the installed returning-service model; it adds no knowledge,
zero-knowledge or native-realization claim. Input-clause interpretations and
nonvacuity evidence remain checked against the fixed subject.

  bound of `CompiledGuessFiatShamir.soundness` for that theorem's verifier
  (`guess_fixed_soundness`), attained at zero queries. Statement determination
  of the adaptive binding is a named hypothesis of the binding category,
  discharged by the codec's proof (`determination_discharged`).
- **Malformed statement.** A one-entry vector at one round with a claim other
  than its total is false under the Sumcheck contract (`malformed_false`),
  denotes no model statement (`malformed_unmapped`), has a doomed original
  validator (`malformed_doomed`), and the fixed-statement application bounds
  acceptance on it (`malformed_soundness`).
- **Wrong validator.** The Schnorr original interacts before it decides, so no
  family whose validator accepts outright meets the model equation
  (`wrong_validator`).
- **Requirements and artifacts.** Lowering evidence is required exactly for
  program bytes and binding evidence exactly for adaptive statements, by the
  evidence's indices; the composed check refuses the Schnorr original with the
  guessing export (`mismatched_artifacts`), so no evidence exists for that pair.

With the subject pipeline's evidence, the assurance reports of schnorr,
chaum-pedersen, schnorr-and, okamoto-proof, guess and sumcheck are
`established`. The composed Sumcheck and GKR Entries retain no target clause. Every
declaration's axioms are within `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.

## Construction obligation counterexamples

`TestsSecurity.Counterexamples` refutes, for each example, the property it
breaks and the formal reading of the obligation it violates
([construction obligations](../../docs/spec/ir/construction-obligations.md)).
Only security attacks quantify the adversary's success, as the probability that
a false statement is accepted in the lazily sampled oracle, or for every
challenge and hash function where stated. The other examples refute a law, a
coupling, a serialization, a construction identity, a proof route, an import
format or the scope of a claim.

the Entry package refuses the substitution with `ring-asset-identity`

**Certificate-absence cases** are classified separately and are not refusal
tests: protocol-level ordering and authentication bugs that no construction
clause refuses and that show only as the impossibility of proving the protocol's
round-by-round certificate. Examples are a last challenge already present in the
interactive source and absorbed evaluations that are never authenticated against
the verifier key. Refusing an unsupported program never counts as detecting
them.
