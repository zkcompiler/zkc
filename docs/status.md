# Status

## Active Core build

The compiler admits named functions and protocols in definition order. On valid inputs satisfying
a function's declared requirements, admission proves its summary and recorded bounds. Protocol
admission checks each role's projected body. The public role-safety theorem excludes an
undefined stop for valid inputs and permitted replies, including calls with checked preconditions.
See the [Core guide](../lean/compiler/Zkc/Core/README.md) and the
[active build targets](development.md).

## Interactive source proofs on typed Core

The frontend emits the protocol into typed Core. Admission checks the program,
and projection gives the process for each role. The formal theorems describe the admitted
entry or its selected reduction call. The source clients check that the typed call and its
emitted attachment select the same operands and function instance. For the table extension
and GKR summand they also check the formal symbol named by that instance's `external` declaration.
Interpreting the symbol in the mathematical model is a separate obligation. The tests exercise
this source path with trusted elaboration and lowering.

| Entry checked | Result and assumptions |
|---|---|
| Sumcheck verifier | A false sum is accepted with probability at most `n/S` for `n` rounds and a challenge set of size `S`, under the arithmetic and reached-challenge laws. The written-charge client also derives the VCVio bound over an answering uniform service from the closed rational ceiling and all emitted draw addresses at 0, 1 and 10 rounds. It checks the deciding role, public operands, sum relation and source table extension. Each executed member may use the verified barycentric or affine interpolation. |
| Honest joint Sumcheck | A valid table of length `2ⁿ` with its true sum returns successfully for every reply under the table and arithmetic laws. Full reply mass at reached calls gives return probability one. |
| GKR layer verifier | At the four-round `Reduce<Gate>` call, a false incoming claim yields a correct residual with probability at most `8/S`, under the gate, reached-challenge and polynomial laws for those four rounds. The issued component binds the source summand, admitted call, gate primitive and four degree-two challenges. Its VCVio bound assumes an interpretation of that summand and the gate and restriction laws. Whole-circuit soundness also needs the wiring, line reduction, layer composition and terminal evaluation. |

Run `just test-interactive` for the source checks, their mutation controls and the
kernel audit. The [formal package guide](../lean/formal/README.md) gives the
theorem entry points and the [source checks](../lean/formal/checks/SourceProtocols.lean)
exercise the certificates.

## Judgment with fees

A process is judged within a budget when, at every effect, a fee chosen before the reply pays for the replies excluded there and every other permitted reply is judged within what is left. The budget is a nonnegative rational. Under the fees of samplers, a fee pays for a set of replies whose probability under the sampler of the effect is at most the fee. One theorem takes the judgment to VCVio's probability for any signature: a process judged within a budget not to return badly returns badly with probability at most the budget, under every service with a state that answers only permitted replies and draws as the samplers do. The run against a service is VCVio's simulation: a process is a computation that asks the oracles of its effects, a service is a handler of their queries with a state and a possible failure, every such handler is the handler of a service, and the run is `simulateQ` of the one by the other (`Zkc.Formal.OracleProcess`). A result about a compiled program is stated about that simulation. Draws are VCVio's uniform sampler of the type drawn. A test on one uniform draw of a field of five elements meets the hypotheses with a service that answers, reaches the bound of one fifth, for the run and for the simulation, and shows that no budget below one fifth judges the process.

A fee can also be held to what its effect is allowed: under the fees within allowances, a fee is paid by the sampler of the effect and is at most the allowance of that effect. A written charge gives the allowance. In a run of the definition that owns it, it allows each effect at one of its draws its ceiling and every other effect nothing; in a run of another definition it allows nothing. Its draws together are allowed its budget, which as a probability is the total that the earlier endpoints state. The allowance is of the event at an address: the role that owns a draw and a role that hears it are allowed the same, and which of them can pay is said by the samplers. The occurrence of a draw is defined where no loop with a count read at run time, no application and no attempt stands around the instruction.

The first admitted subject is `Count<8>` of the reference corpus (N-GL2a), whose two goals give its tolerance the values 4 and 1. `lean/formal/checks/CountCharge.lean` prints the package of what the two goals claim (the protocol of the scheme, then the relation of the clause at each goal's value, as the functions that the frontend emits) and writes beside it what it read from the source as terms: the key of the root, the roles, and for each goal the value of its proof argument, its charge on the draw and the key of its relation. In that module, which imports nothing of the frontend but the record of a charge, the theorems are about every program that admission gives for the stored program of the package. The function of each relation is proved to return whether at most the goal's tolerance of the entries of the table are not zero: its count is one loop, entered by the rule of a loop with an invariant at a symbolic iteration, and its body is walked once. The goals take as their premise that this admitted function returns false on the table. On such a table of eight entries the verifier is judged never to return within the budget of the goal's charge, each fee within what the charge allows the effect that pays it; against every service that draws the position uniformly and answers only what is permitted, the simulation of the verifier by the service's handler returns with probability at most the total of the charge, 3/8 and 3/4. The general theorems are over the standard model of the rows on any field and group data; the charge of each goal is proved equal to the bound at its tolerance. Over the field of two elements, for each goal a table outside the relation and a service meet every hypothesis, and there the simulation returns with probability exactly the total of the charge, so the bound is not met by a program that never returns and no smaller bound is true. A charge on another instruction, and a charge of another definition with a draw at the same key, judge the verifier at no budget, so the fee is paid at the draw of the root. The same text proves the same theorems with the roster reversed, where the verifier is the first role, and Lean refuses five controls, each for the theorems that depend on what it changes: the relation changed, the roles exchanged, a charge below the bound, a charge on another instruction, and a charge of another definition.

The proof walks the verifier's program one step at a time (`walk_step`, `lean/formal/Zkc/Formal/Walk.lean`): the kernel reduces what the next instruction denotes, and the goal that follows is definitionally the old one, on values in a form to read. The same step is taken on a hypothesis, which gives the converse used for the two negative theorems, and on an instruction whose key is no literal. The driver takes the steps of a region without a loop, a block, an application or a call of a function. The body of a function is walked by the same step, under the judgment of a process without effects (`ProcessJudgment.silent`, `functionResult_of_judged`), and a loop is entered by a rule stated for every dialect (`Term.interpret_loop`, `Semantics.interpret_loopStep`) and judged by `Process.interpret_iterate`.

What this does not show. The statement is still written in the text of the check, for one protocol and one size: its premise is the meaning of the admitted relation, and nothing of the relation is bound by hand, but the theorems, the sampler of the draw and the walk of the relation are text written for `Count<8>`. With `<=` of the relation changed to `<`, the deployed definitions and both charges are what they were and the relation is another function; the charge of the first goal, 3/8, is then below the true bound of 4/8, and Lean refuses the theorems that say what the relation computes and those that rest on them. A statement generated from the claims, with the sampler taken from the law of the draw and the comparison of the bound with the ceiling that a goal may ask for, is the next unit. Of other sizes only the charge is read.

## Printed packages

The frontend prints an admitted package as a Lean module that holds the program by names and nothing computed from it. In the module the kernel elaborates each definition against the headers of those before it; the typed definition is the kernel's normal form of what the elaboration returns, with the equation between the two, and the typed program is those definitions in order. The program of any admission of the program by names is then that typed program. The frontend's own elaboration is not read by the module, so a module cannot hold a typed program that differs from what the Core elaborates. The printer has a case for every constructor of the program by names and reads no source identifier. The module imports the Core's admission theorems, the vocabulary of a package and the kernel command, and nothing of the frontend.

The command that asks the kernel to compute (`kernel_ok` of `Zkc.Kernel`) is meta code outside the audited libraries. Each definition and each equation it adds is checked by the kernel like any other; Lean's unifier is not asked to compute. A normal form takes the parameters and the indices of a constructor from the expected type and not from the computation that produced the term, and a proof is stated at the proposition of its field.

A module holds one printed form of a program, so the kernel cannot find a wrong print that still elaborates. `just test-packages` covers that in two ways: the program and the association that Lean read from each module are compared, by value, with those that the frontend gave to admission; and a fixture is printed, read by Lean and compared with the fixture by value. A check computes, from the types of the fixture, every constructor that a program by names, an association and the rows of a project can hold, with the keys of the standard vocabulary and the parameters of a project primitive, and fails when the fixture lacks one; a constructor added later makes `just test-packages` fail until the fixture holds it. The comparison is by constructor, not by field, and numbers and strings are not counted. The fixture is data for the printer and is not elaborated.

`just test-packages` prints every admitted root of seven examples: Schnorr, Chaum–Pedersen, Sumcheck with two rounds and with ten (sixty-five definitions), GKR with its fifteen project primitives, the inner-product argument, a protocol over variants and the fragments. Lean checks each of the fifteen modules, and four controls are refused: a call of a definition that the program does not have, which the kernel command refuses as an elaboration error; a key changed in a definition, which elaborates and which only the comparison with the source refuses; the comparison with the program of another source; and an association with a claim that the package was not printed with, which admits and which only the comparison of the association refuses. The packages of this check are printed with no association of a scheme.

## Written three-message knowledge

The source-issued Schnorr and Chaum–Pedersen knowledge theorem covers the admitted verifier, extractor and relation. The extractor takes a sized sequence of nominal run records for the selected closed protocol. Source-level checks cover the owning contract, explicit roles and available statement fields. Both protocols use the same exact returning-trace characterization, row interpretation laws and group-action proof. An independent companion can prove the Core target directly. Finite-model controls falsify it after deleting a verifier guard or changing a selected function. Oracle names and opening operands survive resolution; full source oracle elaboration is pending. Run `just test-written-knowledge` and `just test-interactive`. See the [proof and model boundaries](written-knowledge.md).

Resolved goals and contracts retain declaration identity and named statement arguments. Ordinary resolution enforces unique contract ownership. The compiler checks its property catalog against the language and library references. A per-use environment closes specification-only numerals independently of the deployed callee. Two uses of `Count<8>` share an admitted verifier and close the declared charge to `3/8` and `3/4` respectively. The emitted returning-record equality compares ordered keys and nominal types. A named `special_soundness` goal at `written` selects a scheme and its knowledge clause. A `soundness` goal at `written` reads the `proves` or `reduces` clause that it names by `clause = label`, or the only one of the contract (GL3): the clause is given as the source wrote it, with its kind, label, deciding role, the relation it applies and the operands in the order of the relation's parameters, and its deciding role is the verifier of the scheme. A label that no clause has, or that a clause of another kind has, refuses the goal. GL3 refuses a goal that names none of several clauses, and a goal on a protocol without a clause of the kind. That is enforced where a claim is formed, not where a goal is read: such a goal still reads the charge at a site, which states no claim, because a caller without a contract reads the charges of its callees through its goal. Extra scheme context remains in the public statement. The independent consumers supply their own role inputs, function arguments and observed addresses. The formal arithmetic model constructs the group interfaces from a Mathlib field action. A separate process interpretation uses VCVio for challenge probabilities. Run `just test-formal` to build and audit the public package.

## Source conformance

`just test-frontend` exercises parsing and formatting across the reference corpus, compares
assembly and name resolution with the expected rules, takes every reference case through the typing of the program and fixes the counts of the outcomes, and checks generated views at closed
protocol instances. It also checks that guarded indexing survives a send at its sender,
requires local evidence at the receiver, and reports the role missing a bound at a shared
binding. Positive cases emit a root and pass Core admission. Resolution checks the statement/witness split of a top-level relation in
`proves`, `reduces` and `through`, the form of knowledge branches, named algorithms and loop references.
Where a `proves` clause or the source of a `reduces` clause applies a relation, the application is typed at the instance of the protocol as an application in an expression is: the static arguments of the relation are inferred from the operand types, a `where` on its head holds at the instance, the operands have the types of its parameters, and its body is typed. Where a goal or an application gives values to specification-only parameters, the `where` is checked at those values and the body of the relation is typed at them, so an index or a size that is a proof argument is checked against the value of each use. A relation or a specification function that a specification item calls is reached at the values of its own specification-only parameters, which it shares with the caller or is given in a specification argument; a call that leaves one out is refused (SP11). A definition generic over a module parameter waits where one place of its text is typed more than once and stands for different functions at an instance: members of two modules, as in a loop whose receiver goes from a value of one module's type member to a value of another's, or a member in one typing and a function of the type's own module in another. The typing at the instance reads one resolution for a place, and before this such a program was admitted and called one function for all (39 written out, 99 as a loop). `just test-formal` runs thirteen such programs, which wait, and nineteen written-out forms and places with one member, which mean what the source says (`Zkc.Tests.Formal.MemberResolution`): dot calls and an operator, functions and a protocol, and module parameters among naturals and under a section. Not changed: a `where` that equates a type member with itself (`where X::T == X::T`) makes the member count as equated with a type, so an operator on a value of it is the function of the type's own module and not the member of the interface. A clause runs nowhere, so an operand or a body of a relation that waits for a later layer of the language leaves the clause untyped and the protocol unaffected; an operand that waits is met before the instance of the relation, so the `where` is then not checked, at a goal either. No clause of the examples is left so: each is typed with its body. The short application of a relation with witness parameters, at the top of a `proves` clause or of the source of a `reduces` clause, is typed on its statement operands (SX3); before this it was refused there. What written soundness goals of one scheme claim is read as one package (`Written.goalClaims`): the definitions of the scheme's protocol first, with the keys and the stored definitions of its own package, then the relation of each goal's clause at the goal's proof values, instantiated in one state so that each has a key of its own; the ports at the statement and at the witness are given by their places. `checks/GoalClaim.lean` compares the stored definitions, and Lean's kernel accepts that the processes of the root's roles in that package are those of the deployed package. The contract of a protocol generic over a module parameter is typed in both typings of SP12, as its body is: a clause reads of a type member what the interface declares, calls the members that a body may call, and the instance keeps the resolutions for the typing at a goal. A specification argument of an application that reads the index of a loop that is one loop for every iteration is refused (SP11); in a loop typed per iteration each application gives its own value. A sum of naturals over a static range is written term by term where its index is an operand of arithmetic in the term, which is TY8's condition, and is one loop where the index is read as a position or in a condition; before this every sum whose term named the index was written term by term, so that the count of a relation over a table of `n` entries was `n` blocks. No deployed function of an example changes. An operand that is no port is not read yet, and a claim whose relation links a primitive that no deployed definition links is not compared with the deployed package. Not typed yet: the relation of a `knowledge` clause, what a clause states under the results or under the index of a loop, a formula that applies no relation, and `requires`. Typing the full contract at each instance remains open. The
[language and frontend review](source-elaboration.md) describes the proposed boundary.
