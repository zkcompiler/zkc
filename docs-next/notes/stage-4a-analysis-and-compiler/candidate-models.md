## 1. Purpose and provisional result

This portfolio compares five materially different integrated Analysis and
Compiler architectures at the same semantic resolution. They are controls,
not a progression in which later candidates automatically supersede earlier
ones:

| Candidate | Required role | Central choice |
|---|---|---|
| A | Preservation control | Enlarge the current closed Soundness kernel and enumerated Compiler |
| B | Completion and alignment control | Federate family-indexed Analysis under a small common lifecycle, then use a staged validation-first Compiler |
| C | Structural-redesign control | Make one universal typed proof/dependency graph the checking and replay substrate while retaining owner-defined semantics |
| D | External-proof-system control | Make exact external proof obligations and adapters the primary Analysis mechanism |
| E | Capability-expanding control | Make checked certificates, relational synthesis, and symbolic finite-domain optimization primary |

The provisional comparative result is:

> Candidate B is the research-leading hypothesis because it shares authority,
> identity, checking, outcome, and replay discipline without pretending that
> trace equivalence, probabilistic closeness, cryptographic properties, costs,
> and property transports have one semantic payload. It may selectively absorb
> mechanisms from Candidates A, C, D, and E. It is not selected until the
> scenario portfolio and cross-cutting matrices close.

Migration cost and current class layout are deliberately excluded from the
comparison.

## 2. Shared comparison axes

Every candidate below answers the same axes:

1. semantic center and owned subjects;
2. question and result families;
3. identity and dependency closure;
4. purpose-specific views, read sets, and adequacy;
5. models, theorems, rules, assumptions, and external correspondence;
6. derivation, direct-check, certificate, and checking authority;
7. qualified outcomes and exact negative meaning;
8. capabilities, persistence, cold replay, and residual trust;
9. equality, trace, distribution, cost, Fiat--Shamir, transport,
   composition, and relation-satisfaction seams;
10. Compiler requests, proposals, target admission, relations, and legality;
11. candidate-domain completeness, constraints, objectives, comparison,
    closed no-eligibility, reports, outer outcomes, and decisions;
12. extension and unknown-question behavior; and
13. Stage 4B and Evidence consumption.

All candidates are interpreted subject to the frozen Stage 3 rules: authority
is process-local; IDs and serialized bytes do not mint it; proposals do not
admit targets; structural relations do not transport properties; and a failed
incomplete search is not a semantic negative.

Every candidate also preserves the declaration/result split for intentional
change. An admitted `ChangeContract` is an unauthoritative declaration of
permitted and protected effects. Only an exact affirmative
`ChangeConforms(source, target, ChangeContract, observer, model, maps)` proposition and
capability establishes that one change conforms. Property composition over an
intentional change requires both the admitted contract and the matching
affirmative conformance capability.

Every candidate realizes the same Analysis identity and profile contract. The
candidate-specific center may be a closed native kernel, a federation, a proof
graph, an external proof environment, or a certificate system, but none may
collapse these categories:

```text
AnalysisQuestion<F>
  exact stable semantic experiment and permitted conclusion shape

AnalysisGoal<F>
  AnalysisQuestionId + one exact hypothesis-free typed conclusion

AnalysisProposition<F>
  AnalysisGoalId + one exact canonical hypothesis context

AnalysisRequest<F>
  one operational attempt to establish an exact proposition or derive a
  conclusion within the question's result schema
```

Only `AnalysisProposition` is truth-apt. `AnalysisGoal` is a non-authoritative
subidentity used to avoid recursive hypothesis naming, and `AnalysisRequest`
is an operational identity. The word *claim* in a candidate description is
only prose or, for Candidate C, the name of a carrier node; it never creates a
fourth Analysis semantic category.

Every candidate also separates the four profile roles:

```text
FamilySemanticProfile<F>
  exact subject, model, experiment, observer/direction, parameter,
  conclusion/refutation, quantitative, and semantic-read meaning

FamilyBasisRegistry<F>
  admitted semantic rules, theorems, proof lanes, ports, and basis extension

FamilyValidationProfile<F>
  checker, decoder, elaborator, translation, proof-rule, and trust contracts

FamilyOperationPolicy<F>
  capability, replay, disclosure, persistence, and unknown-question policy
```

Only the semantic profile enters question, goal, and proposition meaning. A
registry, theorem catalog, checker, implementation, proof bytes, assurance
lane, replay consumer, or operation policy cannot silently change that
meaning. Each candidate must distinguish:

```text
SemanticBasisId
  exact inference/theorem/correspondence/encoding meaning and premise needs

SupportInstantiationId
  the concrete prior JudgmentRecords, live capabilities, and correspondence
  records used to realize those premise needs

ValidationBasisId
  exact checker semantics, implementations, translations, validation-only
  dependency closure, and trust-root DAG

DerivationId
  one direct transcript, derivation DAG, proof, refutation, or certificate

JudgmentRecordId
  exact answered proposition, outcome, support, semantic and validation
  bases, assurance, retained facts, and residual trust

CapabilityBinding and AnalysisReplayBundleId
  respectively one process-local mint and inert consumer-justified replay
  material
```

The semantic basis carries a total dependency-disposition ledger. Every
imported definition, axiom, lemma assumption, theorem assumption, and proof-
environment dependency is classified as an exact established premise, an
exact residual hypothesis inherited into the proposition, or a named
definitional-or-logic trust root. A concrete prior record and its assurance
belong to `SupportInstantiationId`; they do not change the proposition or the
abstract premise requirement. This contract preserves A--E's different
checking centers while preventing any center from hiding semantic assumptions
inside a validator, environment, or generic trust label.

```text
DependencyDisposition =
    EstablishedPremise(exact proposition, polarity, and semantic facts)
  | ResidualHypothesis(exact proposition)
  | DefinitionalOrLogicTrustRoot(exact definition or logic root)
```

Every candidate also uses the following Compiler identity and authority
factorization. The representation and checking mechanism differ by candidate,
but these categories do not:

```text
TransformProblemId
  exact source subjects, transform intent, relation families, directions,
  observers, models, permitted deltas, and required semantic maps

DecisionPolicyId
  exact constraints, objective kinds, accepted qualifications, comparison
  claim, comparator, tie policy, and requested peer-owner inputs

CompileRunRequestId
  one operational invocation of a TransformProblemId and DecisionPolicyId

SearchJobId
  producer, recipe, seed, resources, scheduling, heuristics, and operational
  exploration limits for one run

ProposalScopeId
  one exact scope frozen before admission, relation, assessment, or score
  outcomes are observed

AlternativeResolutionLedgerId
  a total ledger from every scoped pre-admission alternative to an exact
  candidate image, conclusive exclusion, or qualified unresolved result

CandidateDomainId
  one canonical finite domain containing only independently admitted,
  exact-relation-qualified semantic CandidateIds

QualificationId
  one exact affirmative admission and transition support DAG with its
  semantic/validation bases, assurance, and residual trust

QualificationResolutionLedgerId
  one total DecisionPolicy-scoped ledger resolving every CandidateId in D to
  accepted comparison alternatives, definitive qualification ineligibility,
  or an exact undetermined result

ComparisonAlternativeDomainId
  one separately checked-closed finite domain Q of CandidateId plus accepted
  qualification-set alternatives derived from D
```

Producer choice and operational search coordinates never enter
`TransformProblemId`, `DecisionPolicyId`, or semantic `CandidateId`. A proposal
scope becomes a candidate domain only through exact total resolution, checked
duplicate treatment, and a checked canonical-image or symbolic-denotation
result. Proposal-scope coverage proves neither discovery of every legal
transform nor candidate-domain closure unless an additional exact coverage
basis states that claim.

Assessment inputs use one non-circular candidate-indexed contract in every
candidate:

```text
AssessmentInputPortfolio
  immutable body containing concrete qualification, Analysis, peer-owner,
  Stage4B-owned, and Evidence-derived records associated to one CandidateId
  and DecisionPolicyId

AssessmentInputCompletenessResult
  a separate Compiler-owned checked result over the portfolio body and exact
  policy schemas; never a field in the body it checks

AssessmentInputUse
  a checked edge from each still-independently-owned fact to the exact policy
  schema, CandidateId, and portfolio body that consume it

AssessmentLedger
  exactly one decisive assessment or exact unresolved result for every member
  of ComparisonAlternativeDomain Q
```

Thus semantic candidate-domain formation is `D`; qualification resolution
derives the comparison carrier `Q`; and assessment is keyed by `Q`, not merely
by target or candidate. Every closed decision is scoped to exact `D` and `Q`.
Stage 4B, Evidence, and other foreign-owner results enter only through the
portfolio body and checked use edges. Portfolio completeness cannot create,
reinterpret, or absorb those facts, and no foreign result may depend on the
later assessment that consumes it.

Compiler products have three authority classes in every candidate:

```text
QualifiedCompilerDecision
  BestInExactClosedDomain<D,Q>
  CompleteParetoFrontierInExactClosedDomain<D,Q>
  NoEligibleInExactClosedDomain<D,Q>

NonDecisionReport
  QualifiedFeasibleCandidate
  NondominatedInAssessedSubset
  IncompleteSearchOrAssessmentReport

OuterCompilerOutcome
  Unsupported | CannotAnswer | Refused | Malformed | CheckerFailure
```

The complete Pareto result is a closed-domain comparison result, not an
incomplete frontier. Feasible, subset-relative, and incomplete products mint
no `QualifiedCompilerDecision`, domain-closure, optimality, complete-frontier,
or closed-no-eligibility authority.

## 3. Candidate A: extended closed kernel and enumerated Compiler

**Portfolio role:** Preservation control.

### 3.1 Semantic center, subjects, and views

One enlarged native Analysis kernel generalizes the current Soundness Kernel.
Every question is a closed variant of one `AnalysisQuestion`, evaluated against
one immutable catalog and an exact aggregate context:

```text
exact admitted Stage 3 subjects
  + question-variant-specific narrow views
  + immutable native rule catalog
  + explicit derivation plan
  -> tagged AnalysisJudgment | qualified non-result
```

Each question must still cite its exact Protocol, Interface, Plan, relation,
construction, composition, or later occurrence operands. Purpose-specific
views and source-owned adequacy predicates satisfy Stage 3, but one kernel
context remains the center into which all supported facts and rules are
assembled.

### 3.2 Questions, results, identity, and dependencies

The kernel owns a universal closed sum containing variants for:

- Core and Protocol equality;
- observer-indexed trace equality and refinement;
- distributional equality and closeness;
- admitted change declarations, `ChangeConforms`, and cost;
- soundness, knowledge, completeness, and later properties;
- Fiat--Shamir theorem application and property transport;
- property composition; and
- relation satisfaction, assigned to Analysis in this control.

The universal sum is only the kernel's closed representation of the shared
Analysis categories. Each variant supplies a `FamilySemanticProfile`; the
kernel's native rule catalog is its `FamilyBasisRegistry`, its evaluator
contracts form `FamilyValidationProfile`, and its capability/replay rules form
`FamilyOperationPolicy`. A question identifies the exact stable experiment, a
goal adds one exact conclusion, and a proposition adds the canonical residual
hypothesis context. Results answer exact propositions through the corresponding
tagged family result and refutation schemas.

Exact question identity includes the subject tuple, semantic index, model,
observers, direction, maps, parameters, and complete semantic-view closure. It
does **not** include hypotheses, a particular conclusion, the rule catalog,
checker, or derivation plan. The exact catalog slice, native rules,
correspondences, premise propositions, and total dependency-disposition ledger
form `SemanticBasisId`; the evaluator contract, implementation closure, and
trust-root DAG form `ValidationBasisId`; concrete prior records and live
capabilities form `SupportInstantiationId`; and `DerivationId` identifies the
explicit native direct transcript or acyclic derivation plan. Dependency
closure is catalog- and context-wide unless the evaluator checks a smaller
exact slice. Changing a basis, support record, validator, or derivation can
therefore establish the same proposition again without changing its meaning.

### 3.3 Models, external correspondence, and checking authority

Every affirmative or negative semantic conclusion must be represented by a
closed native rule-body variant or complete native decision procedure. An
external theorem enters through a specially authored binding, an explicit
assumed premise, or a native rule body whose admission separately accounts for
the external theorem statement, model correspondence, and dependencies.

A single native evaluator checks direct rules and acyclic derivation DAGs. It
retains the current strengths of closed rule syntax, caller-supplied proof
objects, inherited hypotheses, exact substitution, and exact quantitative
arithmetic. Every imported dependency is totally classified as an established
premise, residual hypothesis, or definitional/logic root; native placement
cannot hide a domain assumption inside validator trust. Proof search remains
separate from validity.

### 3.4 Outcomes, capabilities, replay, and trust

The current result/refusal boundary expands to:

```text
affirmative
fact-retaining negative
unsupported
cannot answer
refused
malformed
checker failure
```

A negative capability is available only from a complete direct procedure or
checked refutation rule that answers an exact negative proposition.
Unsuccessful derivation search is `CannotAnswer`. Completed semantic outcomes
mint one exact process-local kernel capability and one `JudgmentRecordId`
binding the proposition, support instantiation, semantic basis, validation
basis, derivation, outcome, assurance, retained facts, and residual trust.
Persistence, where justified, retains the exact context, catalog slice,
subjects, question, goal, proposition, support, derivation, result, checker
identity, and residual trust; cold replay reconstructs and reruns the kernel.

The native family schemas, semantic-model definitions, and rule catalog are
specified roots. Their adequacy, every direct procedure's claimed soundness or
completeness, and correspondence between the specified evaluator and its
running implementation terminate in named direct or mechanized checks where
available and otherwise remain explicit residual-trust roots. An external
binding additionally retains theorem truth, statement/model correspondence,
and adapter correctness as separately discharged or residual roots. Native
placement does not make any of these obligations self-justifying.

### 3.5 Property seams

Equality, trace, distribution, cost, cryptographic property, FS, transport,
composition, and relation-satisfaction meanings become new rule-body families
inside the kernel. This candidate assigns `RelationSatisfies` to Analysis as a
native family in order to test that ownership alternative. Its question cites
the exact relation definition, instance, interpretation, public assignment,
and one opaque occurrence-local witness capability; secret witness material is
neither globally identified nor persisted. `CheckedFSConstruction` and
`CheckedCoreComposition` remain exact structural premises. Property transport
remains property-indexed even though it shares the evaluator.

### 3.6 Compiler architecture

The current staged evaluator remains recognizable:

```text
FRAME -> RUN -> PROPOSE -> FREEZE-SCOPE -> RESOLVE
  -> FORM-CANDIDATE-DOMAIN -> LEGAL -> ASSESS -> COMPARE
```

Provider output is changed into an unauthoritative proposal, and PIR performs
independent target admission before a family checker establishes the exact
transition. Compiler-local legality is then checked as its own qualified fact;
it cannot stand for PIR admission, transform validity, Analysis, or Stage 4B
feasibility. A Compiler plan still combines transform choices with explicit
Analysis derivation choices. Alternative proof bases are distinguished only
when residual trust is an explicit request dimension; otherwise they establish
the same semantic candidate.

`TransformProblemId` and `DecisionPolicyId` define semantic transformation and
comparison meaning. `CompileRunRequestId` and `SearchJobId` retain the chosen
closed plan, derivation search, producer, and resource envelope only as
operational identity. An exact enumerated or submitted `ProposalScopeId` is
frozen before semantic outcomes are read. Its
`AlternativeResolutionLedgerId` classifies every pre-admission descriptor as
resolved to one or more admitted relation-qualified `CandidateId` values,
conclusively excluded, or qualified unresolved. Only the canonical resolved
image forms `CandidateDomainId`; failed or unavailable resolutions remain in
scope accounting and prevent any closed claim whose scope coverage depends on
them.

Only explicitly enumerated closed candidate domains and exact submitted
candidate sets are supported. Each semantic candidate may have multiple native
`QualificationId` supports. A total `QualificationResolutionLedger` resolves
every member of exact `D` under the decision policy to a canonical accepted
qualification set, a definitive qualification exclusion, or an exact
undetermined result. Its separately checked canonical image is
`ComparisonAlternativeDomain Q`. The kernel then builds an immutable candidate-indexed
`AssessmentInputPortfolio` from the accepted qualification plus exact Analysis,
peer-owner, Stage 4B, and Evidence records. A separate completeness checker and
one `AssessmentInputUse` edge per foreign fact precede the `Q`-keyed assessment
ledger, so the portfolio never certifies itself.

Every member of `Q` is decisively assessed or retains an exact blocker before
a closed `NoEligibleInExactClosedDomain<D,Q>` result. Comparison is
lexicographic and deterministic, but stable comparison-alternative identity
replaces provider ordinal as the default tie-break. Closed best and no-eligible
results over exact `D,Q` are `QualifiedCompilerDecision` values. Feasible or
incomplete products are `NonDecisionReport` values, while unsupportedness,
cannot-answer, refusal, malformation, and checker failure are outer operation
outcomes. Endpoint feasibility enters only as an explicit Stage 4B-owned
portfolio input. Measurements remain independently Evidence-owned portfolio
inputs.

### 3.7 Extension and unknowns

A new family requires widening the central question/result sum, rule catalog,
evaluator, context, identity regime, and replay surface. Unsupported variants
are exact `Unsupported` outcomes rather than false judgments.

### 3.9 Strengths, costs, and failure cases

Strengths:

- smallest conceptual departure from the current model;
- compact native trusted kernel;
- excellent auditability for exact algebraic rule bodies;
- strong deterministic recomputation; and
- naturally rigorous finite enumeration.

Costs and failure cases:

- one result sum becomes semantically heterogeneous;
- traces, subdistributions, adversarial games, costs, and refutations strain
  one derivation algebra;
- external theorem correspondence remains awkward or assumption-heavy;
- Compiler policy continues to choose proof plans;
- aggregate context pressure works against exact family-local read closure;
- central regime changes accompany every material extension; and
- two proofs of the same proposition are easily confused with two semantic
  candidates.

Candidate A is viable as a preservation control but does not offer the cleanest
ideal factorization.

## 4. Candidate B: federated typed Analysis and validated-decision Compiler

**Portfolio role:** Completion and alignment control; research-leading
hypothesis pending validation.

### 4.1 Semantic center, subjects, and views

A small `AnalysisCore` owns the common lifecycle. Each closed
`FamilySemanticProfile<F>` owns its exact semantics, while its basis registry,
validation profile, and operation policy independently govern proof lanes,
checkers, and capability/replay behavior:

```text
AnalysisCore
  + FamilySemanticProfile<F>
  + exact admitted subject tuple
  + exact purpose-specific views and adequacy
  + exact semantic model instance
  + SemanticBasis + SupportInstantiation + ValidationBasis
  -> QualifiedJudgment<F>
```

The common core owns identity separation, dependency closure, basis and
derivation authentication, qualified outcomes, capability discipline, replay,
and only those exact-expression primitives genuinely shared by several
families. It does not own a generic semantic payload.

Each family defines a closed subject grammar. Irrelevant operands are absent,
not optional or defaulted. For example:

```text
TraceEq<O>(
  source Protocol,
  target Protocol,
  observer O,
  trace model,
  occurrence and value maps,
  termination and failure policy)

DistributionClose<M>(
  source Protocol,
  target Protocol,
  probability model,
  input distribution,
  correlation and challenge regime,
  abort and failure mass,
  measurable map,
  metric, direction, and bound)

Soundness<N>(
  Protocol,
  exact relation and occurrence operands when read,
  adversary and oracle model,
  notion N,
  resources,
  assumptions)
```

### 4.2 Question, goal, proposition, basis, support, and record identity

Candidate B separates semantic meaning from the mechanism that establishes it:

```text
QuestionId
  = FamilySemanticProfileId and semantic regime
    + exact subjects and model instantiation
    + observers, direction, maps, parameters, permitted result schema,
      and semantic read closure

GoalId
  = QuestionId + exact hypothesis-free typed conclusion

PropositionId
  = GoalId + exact canonical typed hypothesis context

RequestId
  = QuestionId + exact target proposition or derive-within schema
    + offered bases, requested assurance, operational limits, and optional
      persistence purpose

SemanticBasisId
  = FamilyBasisRegistry slice + rule, theorem, correspondence, encoding,
    query polarity, exact abstract premise requirements, substitutions,
    transformers, and total dependency disposition

SupportInstantiationId
  = SemanticBasisId + concrete premise JudgmentRecordIds, live capabilities,
    correspondence records, and exact dependency-disposition realization

ValidationBasisId
  = SemanticBasisId + FamilyValidationProfile slice + checker contracts and
    implementations + decoder/elaborator/translation/proof-rule closure
    + validation-only reads and trust-root DAG

BasisQualificationId
  = SemanticBasisId + ValidationBasisId + assurance class + residual trust

DerivationId
  = one concrete direct-check transcript, derivation DAG,
    proof object, certificate, or refutation

JudgmentRecordId
  = PropositionId + affirmative or exact-negative outcome
    + SupportInstantiationId + SemanticBasisId + ValidationBasisId
    + BasisQualificationId + DerivationId + retained facts

CapabilityBinding
  = exact successful proposition, qualification, derivation, subjects,
    and local mint

ReplayBundleId
  = complete persisted replay inputs and consumer purpose
```

The four profile roles remain independent: `FamilySemanticProfile` alone
defines question/goal/proposition meaning; `FamilyBasisRegistry` enumerates
semantic lanes and ports; `FamilyValidationProfile` defines checker contracts;
and `FamilyOperationPolicy` defines capability, replay, disclosure, and
unknown-question behavior. Theorem, support record, checker, derivation,
request, capability-mint, and replay identities do not change proposition
meaning merely because they establish, attempt, carry, or replay it. Several
bases may establish the same proposition. Non-semantic outcomes such as
`CannotAnswer`, refusal, malformation, and checker failure produce exact
request/attempt records, not proposition judgments. Every live capability
nevertheless retains its exact checked semantic basis, concrete support,
validation basis, derivation, assurance, and dependencies.

The semantic basis has a total dependency-disposition ledger. Exact prior
judgments are abstract established-premise requirements whose concrete record
selection belongs to `SupportInstantiationId`; every unproved truth-apt
dependency becomes a residual hypothesis in `PropositionId`; only named
definition or logic adequacy may terminate as a trust root. Validator residual
trust cannot absorb theorem assumptions or model correspondence.

### 4.3 Models, rules, proof lanes, and external correspondence

Each family explicitly admits whichever checking lanes it supports:

1. a complete direct decision procedure;
2. an internal typed derivation DAG;
3. an external proof with checked theorem and model correspondence;
4. a solver or proof certificate with exact query and polarity; or
5. a measurement-derived inference only through an explicit model-backed
   Analysis rule.

External use has three independently checkable boundaries:

```text
the external proof checks the exact external statement
the external symbols and model bind to the exact zkc subjects
the external result implies the exact zkc claim in the required direction
```

Missing correspondence yields `CannotAnswer` or an explicitly conditional
claim. A theorem name, repository, proof file, test receipt, or loaded prover
does not satisfy any of these boundaries by itself.

### 4.4 Outcomes, capabilities, replay, and residual trust

Every family defines which outcomes are meaningful:

- affirmative semantic result;
- fact-retaining negative or exact refutation;
- unsupported family, model, or regime;
- cannot answer with the supplied basis;
- refused for missing authority or explicit policy;
- malformed question or basis; and
- checker failure.

A negative capability is minted only by a complete decision procedure or an
exact checked refutation. Invalid certificates and unsuccessful proof search
establish nothing about the semantic negation. A completed result mints only
the exact family-indexed process-local capability.

Persistence exists only for a named independent consumer. Its replay bundle
binds the complete subject, question, model, assumptions, maps, dependencies,
basis, derivation, checker identity, result, and residual trust. Replay
reconstructs and rechecks these inputs; bytes never preserve the live
capability.

Each family profile terminates its trust chain explicitly. Family-schema and
semantic-model definitions are specified roots. Model adequacy, rule
soundness, direct-procedure completeness, theorem applicability, certificate
encoding, and correspondence adapters are established by named direct or
mechanized root checks when available and otherwise remain typed residual-trust
roots. Validation-basis identity separately binds proof/certificate calculus,
query polarity, checker ABI, accepted imports, and correspondence between the
specified checker and its running implementation. Pointing to another adapter,
registry, or proof environment never closes one of these roots.

### 4.5 Family surface and exact property seams

Candidate B admits distinct profiles for:

- `CoreEq` and `ProtocolEq`;
- `TraceEq<O>`;
- directed `TraceRefines<source,target,O>`;
- `ChangeConforms` over an exact admitted `ChangeContract`;
- `DistributionEq` and directional `DistributionClose`;
- exact operational `CostRelation`;
- soundness, knowledge, completeness, and later property profiles;
- FS theorem instantiation and property transport;
- property-specific composition; and
- property coverage over an owner-defined obligation manifest.

A family may have a closed schema but no v0 rule catalog. For example, a
future zero-knowledge profile can be exactly representable while evaluation is
`Unsupported`; it is not thereby false or approximately modeled.

`RelationSatisfies` remains owned by Relations. It interprets one exact
relation definition and instance against one occurrence-local private witness
assignment, which is base relation semantics rather than a derived cross-
Protocol theorem. Analysis consumes the exact result in completeness and
other property questions. The witness capability remains confidential,
occurrence-local, and process-local; no global identity over secret bytes is
required.

Fiat--Shamir is factored as:

```text
CheckedFSConstruction
  // Stage 3 structural result

CheckedFSTheoremInstantiation<T>
  // exact theorem/model applicability, assumptions, and typed property ports

PropertyTransport<P>(exact source P, one exact P port)
  -> exact target P with explicit losses and hypotheses
```

If the durable name `FSCompile` is retained, it denotes the middle operation.
It cannot mean that every property is preserved.

Composition is analogous:

```text
CheckedCoreComposition

  exact child occurrences,
```

### 4.6 Compiler requests, proposals, admission, and relations

Compiler is an assessor and decision owner, not a proposal, Protocol,
relation, or property authority:

```text
TransformProblem + DecisionPolicy
  -> CompileRunRequest + SearchJob
  -> unauthoritative proposals
  -> frozen exact ProposalScope
  -> PIR authentication and independent target admission
  -> exact checked transition relations
  -> total AlternativeResolutionLedger
  -> exact semantic CandidateDomain D
  -> separate compiler-local legality and qualification resolution
  -> total QualificationResolutionLedger
  -> separately closed ComparisonAlternativeDomain Q
  -> candidate-indexed AssessmentInputPortfolio bodies
  -> separate input-completeness results and checked input-use edges
  -> typed Q-keyed candidate assessments
  -> complete or explicitly incomplete assessment ledger over Q
  -> explicit comparison
  -> qualified D,Q-scoped decision | non-decision report | outer outcome
```

Proposal mechanisms may be deterministic or nondeterministic, heuristic,
MLIR-based, e-graph-based, superoptimizer-based, solver-based, verified, or
proof-carrying. They produce no target or relation authority.

`TransformIntent` states the required semantic relation. `ProposalRecipe`
describes one producer method. A recipe does not define valid target meaning.
Every intermediate target in a multi-step proposal is independently admitted,
and every edge is independently checked. A composite semantic relation is
available only through an exact composition rule.

`CompilerLegality` is a closed Compiler-owned profile over an admitted
transition case. It may check request-local transform restrictions, feature
policy, or other Compiler-owned conditions, but cannot reinterpret PIR
admission, a transition relation, an Analysis result, OIR feasibility, or
runtime support. Its affirmative, exact negative, unsupported, refused,
malformed, and checker-failure outcomes remain distinct inputs to assessment.
`DecisionPolicy` declares only the foreign-result schemas and association
rules it will require. Concrete Analysis, Stage 4B, Evidence, and peer-owner
records can enter only after `CandidateId` exists, through that candidate's
immutable assessment-input portfolio and independent checked use edges.

### 4.7 Candidate, assessment, domain, and decision identity

The semantic, policy, operational, scope, candidate, and decision layers remain
distinct:

```text
TransformProblemId
  = exact source subjects + TransformIntent + relation/model/direction
    + protected observations, permitted deltas, and semantic map schemas

DecisionPolicyId
  = exact constraints + typed objectives + accepted qualifications
    + comparison claim + comparator + tie policy + requested peer inputs

CompileRunRequestId
  = TransformProblemId + DecisionPolicyId + one operational invocation

SearchJobId
  = CompileRunRequestId + producer/recipe + seed/resources/scheduling
    + operational exploration limits

DeclaredAlternativeId
  = ProposalScopeId + canonical pre-admission member descriptor

TargetAlternativeId
  = admitted target Protocol identity

TransitionCaseId
  = exact source, target, direction, checked relation claims,
    intentional deltas, and path when semantically read

CandidateId
  = TransformProblemId + TransitionCaseId

QualificationId
  = exact affirmative admission and transition support DAG
    + semantic and validation bases + assurance + residual trust

ComparisonAlternativeId
  = DecisionPolicyId + CandidateId
    + exact accepted canonical qualification set

AssessmentInputPortfolioId
  = DecisionPolicyId + CandidateId
    + exact associated qualification, Analysis, peer-owner, Stage 4B,
      and Evidence record body

AssessmentId
  = DecisionPolicyId + ComparisonAlternativeId
    + AssessmentInputPortfolioId + separate completeness result
    + exact legality, constraint, objective, and decisive dependency outcomes

DecisionId
  = TransformProblemId + DecisionPolicyId + CandidateDomainId
    + ComparisonAlternativeDomainId + exact closure and decision ledger
    + comparison result
```

`ProposalScopeId` identifies one exact submitted set or finite alternative
grammar frozen before admission, relation, constraint, or score outcomes are
observed. `DeclaredAlternativeId` identifies a scope member even when proposal
production, authentication, or admission fails. It is not a Protocol,
transition, or semantic-candidate identity.

`AlternativeResolutionLedgerId` has exactly one entry for every declared
alternative. An entry is resolved to a canonical nonempty set of
`CandidateId` values, conclusively excluded by an exact admission/relation fact,
or qualified as unsupported, cannot-answer, refused, malformed,
checker-failed, or incomplete. Only resolved admitted and relation-qualified
`CandidateId` values enter `CandidateDomainId`. Unresolved entries remain in
scope accounting and prevent any closed decision whose claimed scope coverage
depends on them. Total proposal-scope resolution does not prove that the
producer discovered every legal transform unless a separate exact exploration
or grammar-coverage result establishes that claim.

Raw proposal identity does not normally identify a semantic candidate.
Proof-basis identity does not change a transition claim, although a policy may
assess alternative qualifications separately when residual trust is an
explicit dimension. The same target through different paths remains distinct
when properties or costs read the path. Otherwise deduplication requires an
explicit checked quotient policy over every affected consumer.

Candidate B supports four exact closed domain forms:

```text
SubmittedCandidateSet
ResolvedSubmittedProposalScope
EnumeratedClosedCandidateDomain
CertifiedSymbolicCandidateDomain
```

Each form denotes only admitted relation-qualified `CandidateId` values.
`SubmittedCandidateSet` begins with exact semantic candidates;
`ResolvedSubmittedProposalScope` begins with frozen pre-admission descriptors
and supplies a candidate domain only after checked total resolution and
canonical-image formation. `CheckedDomainClosure`
establishes a symbolic domain's exact candidate denotation, finiteness,
membership, canonicalization, duplicate rule, and closure. A
separate `CheckedInfeasibility` or `CheckedOptimality` certificate may establish
a decision claim over that already exact domain; neither certificate silently
defines or repairs domain closure.

In v0, `CertifiedSymbolicCandidateDomain` is only a compressed encoding of a
canonical, already materialized, independently PIR-admitted, and transition-
qualified `CandidateId` image. Every member is already named. Symbolic
denotation can compress reconstruction, repeated assessment, or comparison;
it cannot materialize, admit, or qualify an unnamed target. A stronger lazy or
universally quantified domain would require an explicit future Stage 3
reopening and is not a capability of any v0 candidate.

Candidate-domain closure produces exact semantic domain `D`, not the final
comparison carrier. Candidate B then applies the decision policy's
qualification-resolution rule to every member of `D`:

```text
QualificationResolutionEntry =
    ResolvedTo(exact nonempty canonical comparison alternatives)
  | DefinitivelyQualificationIneligible(exact completed policy fact)
  | Undetermined(exact missing, incomplete, unsupported, refused, malformed,
                 or checker-failed qualification support)

QualificationResolutionLedger
  one and only one entry for every CandidateId in D

ComparisonAlternativeDomain Q
  the separately checked canonical image of CandidateId plus accepted
  qualification-set alternatives under DecisionPolicyId
```

Only a checked total ledger and separate comparison-domain closure capability
permit a closed comparison. An incomplete support enumeration is
`Undetermined`, not qualification ineligibility. The same candidate may yield
several comparison alternatives only under the explicit
`CompareQualifiedAlternatives` policy; each alternative still projects to
exactly one `CandidateId`.

Their claims remain distinct:

- `BestInEnumeratedClosedDomain<D,Q>`;
- `BestInSubmittedCandidateSet<D,Q>`; and
- `BestInCertifiedSymbolicDomain<D,Q>`.

An incomplete search may return exact admitted feasible candidates or an
`IncompleteSearchReport`, including a qualified partial Pareto frontier, but
cannot produce a closed no-eligible result or optimality authority.
`NoEligibleInExactClosedDomain<D,Q>` requires complete qualification
resolution over exact `D` and complete eligibility classification of every
comparison alternative in exact `Q`, or an exact checked infeasibility basis
over those same closed domains. A failed admission,
unavailable relation, unavailable legality result, or unavailable Analysis
basis cannot be silently removed from a proposal scope or candidate domain
whose closure claim depended on resolving or assessing it.

### 4.8 Constraints, objectives, comparison, and replay

For every `CandidateId`, Candidate B constructs an immutable
`AssessmentInputPortfolio` body containing exactly the accepted qualification
records and concrete Analysis, peer-owner, Stage 4B-owned, and Evidence-derived
records required by the policy schemas, plus exact association material. The
body cannot contain its own completeness judgment. A separate Compiler-owned
`AssessmentInputCompletenessResult` checks schema coverage, uniqueness,
association, polarity, assurance, and residual-trust acceptance over that exact
body. Each foreign-owner record remains independent; one checked
`AssessmentInputUse` edge states how its exact subject tuple satisfies the
policy schema for this candidate and portfolio. Only those three pieces feed a
`CandidateAssessment` for one member of `Q`. `AssessmentLedgerId` covers every
and only comparison alternative in `Q`, while the qualification-resolution
ledger separately accounts for every candidate in `D`. This ordering prevents
Stage 4B or Evidence from depending on an assessment that already embeds the
same result.

Constraints consume exact typed capabilities. Affirmative constraints require
affirmative capabilities. An exact negative may satisfy only an explicitly
negative constraint. Unsupported, cannot-answer, refusal, malformed, and
failure outcomes cannot be cast to either polarity.

Objectives preserve their epistemic kind:

```text
ExactStructuralValue
ModelDerivedAnalysisValue
EvidenceDerivedEstimate
Stage4BOwnedValue
```

Different kinds are comparable only under an explicit objective policy. A
measurement never becomes a semantic cost theorem. Endpoint feasibility
enters only as a named Stage 4B-owned constraint. Its absence is not a hidden
rejection rule.

Comparison uses one closed, request-selected profile:

```text
LexicographicOrder
ConstrainedMinimum
WeightedAggregate with exact units and normalization
ParetoFrontier
```

These profiles make different claims and are never coerced into one another.
A single-winner profile resolves a remaining tie by stable canonical semantic
candidate identity unless priority is an explicit objective. A Pareto profile
returns the exact nondominated set and selects no winner without a further
explicit policy.

Qualified Compiler decisions form a closed sum over one exact closed candidate
domain `D` and exact closed comparison-alternative domain `Q`:

```text
SelectedBestInClosedDomain<D,Q>
CompleteParetoFrontierInClosedDomain<D,Q>
NoEligibleInClosedDomain<D,Q>
```

Open or incomplete work instead returns a non-decision report:

```text
QualifiedFeasibleCandidate
NondominatedInAssessedSubset
IncompleteSearchOrAssessmentReport
```

Unsupported request/profile, cannot-answer, refusal, malformation, and checker
or infrastructure failure are outer Compiler-operation outcomes. Neither a
non-decision report nor an outer outcome mints `QualifiedCompilerDecision`,
candidate-domain closure, complete-frontier, optimality, or closed-no-
eligibility authority.

Cold replay checks the request, every declared alternative, exact `D` and its
closure basis, admitted targets, transition and legality bases, all candidate
qualifications, the total qualification-resolution ledger, exact `Q` and its
closure, portfolio bodies, separate completeness and use results, Analysis and
foreign-owner bases, `Q`-keyed assessments, comparison profile, and tie rule.
It does not rerun a mutable discovery engine.

## 5. Candidate C: universal Claim IR proof substrate and transform graph

**Portfolio role:** Structural-redesign control.

### 5.1 Semantic center, subjects, and views

All Analysis propositions, transform relations, constraints, and decisions are
carried in one typed `ClaimIR` proof substrate. `ClaimIR` does **not** redefine
their semantics: each proposition constructor cites the exact family- or
upstream-owner definition and its versioned interpretation. Proofs and Compiler
transitions form one content-addressed DAG:

```text
exact Stage 3 subject and view references
  -> ClaimIR propositions
  -> proof, refutation, or certificate edges
  -> independently admitted target nodes
  -> proof-carrying decision node
```

Claim nodes cite Stage 3 subjects rather than duplicating Protocol. They encode
the exact observer, direction, owner-defined semantic model, maps, hypotheses,
read closure, and result proposition. `ClaimIR` is therefore a universal proof
and dependency carrier, not a second universal semantic owner.

Every Analysis constructor is generated from one exact
`FamilySemanticProfile`; the admitted inference-edge schemas come from its
`FamilyBasisRegistry`, graph-edge validation contracts from its
`FamilyValidationProfile`, and graph capability/replay behavior from its
`FamilyOperationPolicy`. Sharing a graph never merges those profile roles.

The rejected alternative is to let `ClaimIR` define Protocol, relation, OIR, or
family semantics itself. That would require a Stage 3 reopening covering every
displaced owner, view, identity, and consumer. This candidate records that
alternative as a falsifying sovereignty expansion and does not adopt it.

### 5.2 Questions, results, identity, and dependencies

Every family has distinct question, goal, and proposition constructors in one
carrier logic. An Analysis proposition node contains one exact
`AnalysisPropositionId`; a `ClaimIR` claim node is merely its typed carrier and
never a replacement Analysis semantic identity. Results are checked proof, checked
refutation, or unknown, with outer unsupported, refusal, malformation, and
checker-failure outcomes. Question, goal, proposition, request, semantic-basis,
support-instantiation, validation-basis, derivation/refutation, judgment,
capability, replay, dependency, and graph-root nodes are separately typed and
content-addressed. Multiple support, validation, or proof nodes may establish
the same proposition node.

Views and their adequacy obligations are explicit proposition operands. A
graph edge cannot widen a view without an owner-created adequacy result.

### 5.3 Models, external correspondence, and checking authority

Native family semantic models and rule schemas are referenced by exact
owner-defined constructors; the proof logic encodes only their typed use and
inference edges. External proof systems either translate a proof into `ClaimIR`
or produce a checked foreign-proof node plus exact statement- and model-
correspondence edges.

A single proof-graph kernel checks all edge kinds and mints exact live node
capabilities. Persisted graph bytes are only replay inputs. Checker correctness,
foreign proof checkers, correspondence adapters, and model adequacy remain
explicit residual trust.

Semantic inference/theorem/correspondence/query edges form
`SemanticBasisId`; selected prior judgment nodes and live capabilities form
`SupportInstantiationId`; graph-kernel, decoder, translation, and foreign-
checker contracts form `ValidationBasisId`. The semantic subgraph contains a
total dependency-disposition edge for every imported dependency, classifying
it as an established premise, inherited residual hypothesis, or named
definitional/logic root. Graph reachability cannot turn an undisclosed theorem
assumption into validation trust.

Trust-root nodes do not justify themselves by graph reachability. Every family
schema and semantic-model definition is a specified root. Model adequacy, rule
soundness, any direct-procedure completeness theorem, foreign statement/model
correspondence, certificate/query encoding, graph-kernel correctness, and
correspondence between each specified checker and running implementation end in
a named direct or mechanized root node or a typed explicit residual-trust root.
An unchecked edge to another graph or proof system does not terminate the
chain.

### 5.4 Property and relation seams

completeness.

  bundle.

The external system's native result algebra is translated to one exact zkc
family result. Absence, timeout, tactic failure, and invalid proof are
`CannotAnswer` or checker failures, never semantic negatives.

### 6.3 Authority, correspondence, replay, and trust

An external checker establishes theorem truth only in its exact external
model. A separate zkc correspondence checker establishes that the external
statement and model suffice for the exact zkc claim. Live zkc authority is
minted only after both checks.

The external theorem statement, zkc implication direction, exact encoding,
abstract premise requirements, and model correspondence live in
`SemanticBasisId`. The selected prior judgment records and capabilities live in
`SupportInstantiationId`. External and correspondence checker semantics,
implementations, translations, imports used only for validation, and trust
roots live in `ValidationBasisId`. Every imported assumption is totally
disposed as an established premise, residual proposition hypothesis, or named
definitional/logic root. Calling an item an external axiom or environment
dependency cannot move a truth-apt assumption into validator trust.

Cold replay reconstructs the exact external environment, proof checker,
translation, correspondence basis, and zkc subjects. Toolchain and theory
stability therefore become part of the replay and residual-trust problem.

The external environment is not the root of zkc meaning. zkc family schemas and
semantic-model definitions remain specified roots. External-logic consistency,
imported axioms, theorem truth, translation/model adequacy, implication
direction, any claimed decision-procedure completeness, proof-checker
correctness, and correspondence between specified adapters/checkers and their
running implementations each terminate in a named direct or mechanized check
or an explicit residual-trust root. Chaining external translations without one
such terminus is an unresolved basis, not a checked correspondence.

### 6.4 Property seams

Equality, trace, refinement, distribution, cost, cryptographic properties, FS,
property transport, and composition are formalized in whichever external logic
supports their exact zkc-native family meaning. Distinct systems may own
different proof bases. This control assigns `RelationSatisfies` to Analysis and
requires a confidential adapter that consumes one opaque occurrence-local
witness capability without exposing or globally identifying secret material.
zkc still defines the exact source and target proposition meanings so
apparently similar theorems from different logics are not silently equated.

### 6.5 Compiler architecture

Compiler gives the exact transformation and comparison meanings distinct
`TransformProblemId` and `DecisionPolicyId` values. One
`CompileRunRequestId` and `SearchJobId` retain the selected external project,
producer, tactic, resources, and environment only as operational identity.
Every declared alternative belongs to a `ProposalScopeId` frozen before proof,
admission, relation, or score outcomes are observed.

Compiler then emits exact external verification obligations for target
admission orchestration, every candidate transition, compiler-local legality,
constraint, objective, and selected property. PIR alone performs target
admission. `AlternativeResolutionLedgerId` maps every frozen scope member to a
conclusive exclusion, qualified unresolved result, or canonical nonempty set of
independently admitted, external-proof-qualified semantic `CandidateId` values.
Only the checked resolved image forms `CandidateDomainId`; an unavailable
external project remains in scope accounting and blocks any closed scope claim
that depends on it.

Proposals may carry external proofs or replayable proof projects, but their
absence establishes no semantic negative. This control supports enumerated
closed candidate domains and exact submitted candidate sets. Selection is
deterministic under one closed comparator profile. After candidate domain `D`
closes, each exact affirmative admission/transition proof DAG and its bases,
assurance, and residual trust forms one `QualificationId`. A total external-
obligation `QualificationResolutionLedger` resolves
every candidate to an accepted exact proof-support alternative, a definitive
qualification rejection, or an exact undetermined result. Its separately
checked canonical image forms `ComparisonAlternativeDomain Q`.

Exact adapters then populate one immutable candidate-indexed
`AssessmentInputPortfolio` body with concrete qualification, Analysis, Stage
4B, Evidence, and other peer-owner records. A separate Compiler proof checks
portfolio completeness and association, and an `AssessmentInputUse` edge
retains the exact foreign fact, adapter, candidate, and policy schema without
absorbing its authority. The assessment ledger covers every and only member of
`Q`. A qualified `NoEligibleInExactClosedDomain<D,Q>` still requires complete
qualification resolution and exhaustive comparison-alternative
classification. Exact `D,Q`-scoped best, complete-Pareto, and no-eligible
results are qualified decisions. Feasible, assessed-subset frontier, and
incomplete products are non-decision reports; unsupported, cannot-answer,
refusal, malformation, and infrastructure failure are outer outcomes.

### 6.6 Extension and unknowns

A family is supported only when one external environment, exact translation,
and correspondence checker cover it. Otherwise it is `Unsupported`. Adding a
new prover requires a new adapter, environment identity contract,
correspondence argument, replay story, and residual-trust account.

### 6.8 Strengths, costs, and failure cases

Strengths:

- maximum leverage from mature theorem and cryptographic reasoning systems;
- avoids rebuilding every sophisticated logic internally;
- strong proof-object stories where the external system supplies them; and
- natural fit for deep family-specific theorems.

Costs and failure cases:

- translation and model adequacy become the dominant TCB;
- two external systems can assign different meanings to similar claims;
- environment and toolchain identity become pervasive;
- cold replay can require large, unstable environments;
- simple structural questions become unnecessarily expensive;
- prover failure is easily confused with semantic failure; and
- zkc cannot fully delegate proposition meaning, so a native exact family
  model remains necessary anyway.

Candidate D is strongest as a set of checked basis adapters inside Candidate B,
not as the semantic sovereign.

## 7. Candidate E: certified relational synthesis and symbolic optimization

**Portfolio role:** Capability-expanding control.

### 7.1 Semantic center, subjects, and views

Compiler is specified as a relational synthesis problem:

```text
admitted source
  + permitted transition relation
  + typed constraints and objectives
  + exact symbolic encoding of an already materialized, PIR-admitted,
    transition-qualified finite CandidateId image
  -> transition, property, domain-reconstruction, and optimality certificates
```

Analysis remains family-indexed, but certificate validation is its primary
mechanism rather than one prescribed derivation calculus. Every certificate
binds exact Stage 3 subjects, views, model, maps, assumptions, query, polarity,
and dependency closure.

Each family still supplies a `FamilySemanticProfile`; certificate schemas and
semantic reductions live in `FamilyBasisRegistry`; certificate decoders and
checkers live in `FamilyValidationProfile`; and capability, replay, disclosure,
and unsupportedness behavior lives in `FamilyOperationPolicy`. Making
certificates primary does not move checker or operation policy into proposition
meaning.

### 7.2 Questions, results, identity, and authority

Questions, goals, propositions, and results retain exact family-specific
semantics. Identities separate question, hypothesis-free goal, conditional
proposition, operational request, semantic basis, support instantiation,
validation basis, exact query/polarity, certificate language, proof object,
checker, derivation, judgment, local capability, and replay bundle. Absence of
a certificate is `CannotAnswer` when a supported certificate lane was attempted
and `Unsupported` when no exact lane exists; an invalid certificate is a
basis/checker failure and does not refute the proposition.

Small independent checkers validate:

- transition certificates;
- property and transport certificates;
- domain membership and closure certificates;
- infeasibility certificates; and
- optimality certificates.

Completed checks mint process-local capabilities. Portable certificate bytes
support cold replay but carry no authority before rechecking.

Every certificate lane has an explicit root ledger. Family-schema and
semantic-model definitions remain zkc specified roots. Model adequacy,
certificate-calculus soundness, exact zkc-to-query encoding and polarity,
solver-to-certificate extraction, any claimed refutation or domain
completeness, checker correctness, and correspondence between the specified
checker and its running implementation terminate in named direct or mechanized
checks where available and otherwise remain explicit residual-trust roots. A
small checker or valid certificate does not establish the adequacy of an
unrooted encoding.

The calculus, exact query and polarity, semantic correspondence, and abstract
premise requirements form `SemanticBasisId`; concrete prior judgment records
and live capabilities form `SupportInstantiationId`; decoder/checker semantics,
implementations, and validation-only dependencies form `ValidationBasisId`.
The semantic basis totally disposes every imported dependency as an established
premise, a residual hypothesis inherited into `PropositionId`, or a named
definitional/logic root. Certificate-checker trust cannot absorb an unproved
domain assumption.

### 7.3 Property and relation seams

Every equality, refinement, distribution, cryptographic, FS, transport, and
composition result requires a certificate language that matches its exact
semantics. There is no generic preservation certificate. Relations owns
`RelationSatisfies`; this candidate supplies an optional certificate checker for
that owner, consuming one opaque occurrence-local witness capability without
publishing or persisting witness material. Equality-saturation evidence applies
only to exact equivalence relations; refinement and `ChangeConforms` over an
admitted `ChangeContract` require separately typed directed certificates.

### 7.4 Compiler architecture

Search may be parallel, heuristic, nondeterministic, e-graph-based,
superoptimizer-based, SMT-backed, MLIR-based, or implemented by a verified
transformer. Search results remain unauthoritative until target admission and
certificate checks complete. `TransformProblemId` and `DecisionPolicyId`
identify semantic transformation and comparison meaning, while
`CompileRunRequestId` and `SearchJobId` retain producer, synthesis strategy,
seed, resource, and scheduling coordinates only as operational identity. Every
declared symbolic or submitted alternative belongs to one exact
`ProposalScopeId` frozen before certificate, admission, relation, constraint,
or score outcomes are observed. PIR admission, family-specific transition
certificates, lineage/maps, and compiler-local legality certificates are
separate; no certificate kind substitutes for another.

`AlternativeResolutionLedgerId` maps every frozen scope member to conclusive
exclusion, a qualified unresolved result, or a canonical nonempty set of
independently admitted, relation-qualified semantic `CandidateId` values. Only
that checked canonical image forms `CandidateDomainId`. Certificate-backed
proposal-scope coverage, legal-transform discovery completeness, symbolic
candidate-domain denotation/closure, infeasibility, and optimality are distinct
claims even when one certificate format can encode each of them.

A v0 symbolic finite set is only a compressed representation of a canonical
image whose every member is already named by `CandidateId`, whose target
material is already materialized and independently PIR-admitted, and whose
transition is already qualified. Its denotation, finiteness, membership,
canonicalization, duplicate rule, and closure are independently checked first.
A separate optimality certificate then proves the exact comparison claim.
Symbolic reconstruction can avoid re-enumerating or rechecking repeated facts;
it cannot create a target, admit it, qualify a transition, or grant candidate
authority to an unnamed alternative. A lazy or universally quantified domain
over unnamed or unadmitted targets is deferred and requires an explicit future
Stage 3 reopening. Submitted sets and explicit enumeration remain separate
domain kinds.

After symbolic or explicit candidate-domain `D` closes, every exact affirmative
admission/transition certificate DAG with its semantic and validation bases,
assurance, and residual trust forms one `QualificationId`. A total certified
`QualificationResolutionLedger` resolves every candidate to accepted exact
support alternatives, definitive qualification ineligibility, or an exact
undetermined result. Its separately checked canonical image forms comparison
domain `ComparisonAlternativeDomain Q`. No optimizer certificate can repair an incomplete ledger or grant
`Q` closure.

Each candidate's exact qualification, Analysis, Stage 4B, Evidence, and other
peer-owner certificate results enter an immutable candidate-indexed
`AssessmentInputPortfolio` body. A distinct Compiler certificate validates
portfolio completeness and association; an `AssessmentInputUse` edge retains
the exact foreign fact and policy-schema association. The assessment ledger is
keyed by every and only member of `Q`, so neither a portfolio nor its foreign
inputs depend on the later assessment they support.

`NoEligibleInExactClosedDomain<D,Q>` requires complete qualification resolution
plus a complete assessment vector over `Q`, or a separate exact infeasibility
certificate over those already checked-closed domains.

Exact `D,Q`-scoped best, complete-Pareto, and no-eligible certificates may
mint `QualifiedCompilerDecision`. Incomplete search may still return a
`QualifiedFeasibleCandidate`, `NondominatedInAssessedSubset`, or
`IncompleteSearchOrAssessmentReport`; these are non-decision reports and cannot
return domain closure, optimality, complete-frontier, or closed-no-eligibility
authority. Unsupported request/certificate family, cannot-answer, refusal,
malformed input, and checker or infrastructure failure are outer operation
outcomes.

Stage 4B feasibility certificates and Evidence-derived objectives remain
separately typed owner inputs carried through the portfolio body, independent
use edges, and separate completeness result; neither is hidden inside synthesis
success.

A new transform or property family requires a new exact certificate language
and checker, or a verified reduction to an existing one. An unsupported family
cannot be approximated through a generic solver-success flag.

### 7.7 Strengths, costs, and failure cases

Strengths:

- supports broad proposal search plus certificate-compressed reconstruction and
  comparison over already materialized finite candidate domains;
- completely separates search from trust;
- supports portfolio optimizers and nondeterministic engines;
- can establish exact finite-domain optimality without re-enumerating or
  repeatedly rechecking every already materialized candidate; and
- provides a strong proof-carrying compilation path.

Costs and failure cases:

- certificate languages are domain- and family-specific;
- symbolic domains can hide incomplete search unless their denotation and
  closure are exact, and v0 therefore forbids unnamed or unadmitted members;
- an optimality checker can approach optimizer complexity;
- equality-saturation mechanisms do not generalize to refinement or
  `ChangeConforms` for a declared intentional change;
- cryptographic-property and cost certificates may dominate transform
  certificates in difficulty;
- invalid certificates establish no semantic negative; and
- demanding certificates for simple complete direct checks adds needless
  machinery.

Candidate E is strongest as optional proposal, relation-checking, and domain-
closure lanes inside Candidate B rather than a universal requirement.

## 10. Recommended synthesis under validation

The current research-leading synthesis hypothesis is:

```text
Candidate B family-indexed semantic architecture and validated-decision planes
  + optional family-local mechanisms drawn from A, C, D, and E
```

Nothing in that formula selects a winner or makes every borrowed mechanism
mandatory. Under the hypothesis, the **mandatory** commitments would be:

- zkc-native family-specific proposition meaning under a lifecycle-only common
  core;
- exact question, goal, proposition, request, semantic basis, support
  instantiation, validation basis, derivation, judgment, capability, and replay
  identity separation, with total dependency disposition;
- separate family semantic, basis-registry, validation, and operation profiles;
- closed semantic family, outcome, relation, legality, objective, comparator,
  and decision profiles;
- separate transform-problem, decision-policy, operational run/search,
  pre-admission proposal-scope, alternative-resolution, semantic candidate,
  qualification-resolution, comparison-alternative, portfolio body/completeness/
  use, assessment, candidate-domain `D`, comparison-domain `Q`, and decision
  identities;
- PIR-owned target admission, exact owner-defined transition relations, and
  separate Compiler-local legality; and
- explicit domain closure, typed policy, qualified decision, and local-only
  authority.

The **optional, family- or request-local** mechanisms would be:

- Candidate A's closed rule bodies, exact algebra, and derivation DAGs only for
  profiles that declare an internal lane;
- Candidate C's dependency/replay graph only as a carrier for owner-defined
  meanings, never as universal semantic sovereignty;
- Candidate D's external proof and correspondence adapters only for profiles
  with an exact checked adapter and acceptable replay/trust boundary;
- Candidate E's certificate checkers, symbolic-domain closure,
  infeasibility/optimality proofs, and arbitrary proposal engines only when the
  request selects those exact lanes, with v0 symbolic domains restricted to
  already materialized, PIR-admitted, transition-qualified `CandidateId`
  images; and
- replay persistence only for a named independent consumer.

Its high-level flow is:

```text
FamilySemanticProfile<F>
  + exact admitted subjects and family-owned views
  + exact semantic model
  -> AnalysisQuestion<F>
  + exact typed conclusion
  -> AnalysisGoal<F>
  + exact canonical hypothesis context
  -> AnalysisProposition<F>
  + SemanticBasis + SupportInstantiation + ValidationBasis
  + direct, internal, external, or certificate derivation
  -> checked family-specific proposition judgment
  -> QualifiedJudgment<F>

TransformProblem + DecisionPolicy
  -> CompileRunRequest + SearchJob
  -> unauthoritative proposals
  -> frozen ProposalScope
  -> PIR-owned target admission
  -> exact checked transition relations
  -> total AlternativeResolutionLedger
  -> exact admitted relation-qualified CandidateDomain D
  -> separate Compiler-local legality and candidate qualification
  -> total QualificationResolutionLedger
  -> separately closed ComparisonAlternativeDomain Q
  -> candidate-indexed AssessmentInputPortfolio bodies
  -> separate completeness results and checked input-use edges
  -> exact typed constraints and objectives
  -> complete assessment ledger over Q
  -> exact comparison profile
  -> QualifiedCompilerDecision<D,Q>
       | NonDecisionReport
       | OuterCompilerOutcome
```

This remains a hypothesis. Scenario and matrix work must still determine:

- whether its family boundaries are sufficient and the common lifecycle is
  genuinely small;
- the exact v0 family and proof-lane catalog;
- the smallest shared quantitative-expression surface;
- which comparator and symbolic-domain profiles earn v0 support;
- whether Relations or Analysis owns final `RelationSatisfies` semantics;
- which independent consumers justify Analysis or decision replay bundles;
- the accepted checker, adapter, model-adequacy, and certificate residual-trust
  boundaries; and
- whether Compiler consumer pressure requires reopening any exact Analysis
  subject, view, or result.

These are visible convergence questions, not defaults hidden by the word
“hybrid.”

## 11. Design cautions for validation

1. Do not implement the common Analysis envelope as one record full of
   optional fields. Use family-indexed constructors with structurally absent
   irrelevant operands.
2. Do not include theorem, derivation, proof file, or checker identity in
   semantic proposition identity. Keep proposition meaning separate from proof
   basis.
3. Do not let Compiler choose an Analysis derivation as part of candidate
   semantic identity unless basis trust is an explicit policy dimension.
4. Do not let domain construction silently drop failed admissions, unavailable
   relations, or unavailable analyses. Every declared alternative needs an
   exact resolution entry, and only admitted relation-qualified candidates may
   enter semantic candidate domain `D`. Then resolve every candidate's accepted
   qualification support before forming separately checked comparison domain
   `Q`; never assess raw `D` as though proof-basis policy were already settled.
5. Do not rerun a mutable proposal engine for cold replay. Recheck the exact
   frozen proposal scope, resolution basis, candidate-domain closure, proposals
   or certificates, admitted targets, relations, judgments, qualification
   resolution, `Q` closure, portfolio bodies, separate completeness/use
   results, and comparison inputs.
6. Do not let enumeration ordinal break ties. Use stable candidate identity or
   an explicit priority objective.
7. Do not turn `FSCompile` into a generic preservation theorem. It instantiates
   an exact theorem/model and exposes only typed property ports.
8. Do not treat measurements as semantic cost judgments. Preserve their
   Evidence origin, model, uncertainty, and reliance policy.
9. Do not globally identify private witness assignments. Relation satisfaction
   retains an occurrence-local confidential capability.
10. Do not permit dynamic family callbacks, registries, proof systems, or
    solver success flags to become semantic authority.
11. Do not infer a composite transition or transported property merely from a

## 12. Reversal triggers

Candidate B should lose its research-leading status if scenario and matrix
work demonstrates any of the following at equal resolution:

1. a genuinely small universal logic encodes every selected family without
   hiding model, observer, abort, adversary, resource, negative, or residual-

2. one external proof environment represents every zkc-native question with
   fully checked model correspondence and acceptable identity and replay

   mixed direct, internal, external, and certificate lanes;
4. cross-family theorem reuse dominates family-local checking so strongly that
   federation creates more semantic duplication than it prevents;
5. family-indexed questions cannot support a clean common capability, outcome,
   or replay lifecycle without recreating an implicit universal logic;
6. exact Compiler scenarios require Analysis basis selection to be part of
   semantic candidate identity rather than an independently assessed input; or
7. a frozen Stage 3 authority, identity, view, or replay rule creates an exact
   contradiction that Candidate B cannot resolve without a chartered reopening.

supported proof or Compiler lanes while the semantic architecture remains
