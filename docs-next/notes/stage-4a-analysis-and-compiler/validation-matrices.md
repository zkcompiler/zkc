## 1. Purpose

The scenario portfolio asks whether one concrete flow is safe and useful. The
matrices below test whether the same distinctions remain stable across every
family, proof lane, producer, consumer, process boundary, and extension.

The matrices are target hypotheses, not wire schemas or implementation class
designs. Names may change during convergence. The required separations may not
be collapsed.

## 2. Verdict interpretation

| Verdict | Exact interpretation | Selection consequence |
|---|---|---|
| `P` — Pass | The written candidate closes the scenario with the exact qualified outcome, authority, capability, consumer behavior, and replay boundary. | Positive validation evidence within the scenario's exact scope. It is not an implementation or theorem claim. |
| `C` — Conditional | The architecture can preserve the contract only after a named family model, theorem/model adapter, certificate language, checker, or closure proof is instantiated. Until then it must return `Unsupported` or `CannotAnswer`. | An open whole-Stage obligation if the capability is required; otherwise an honest bounded deferral. |
| `S` — Safe capability gap | The candidate deliberately does not offer the positive capability and returns a precise non-result without laundering authority. | Safety pass, capability failure. It cannot be counted as satisfying a required or opportunity scenario. |
| `F` — Structural failure | The candidate's written identity, authority, outcome, or consumer contract permits a falsifier or cannot express the required distinction without material redesign. | Candidate must be repaired and re-audited, or the alternative is rejected for that role. |

Verdict counts are diagnostic, not votes. A candidate cannot win by declaring
every difficult family unsupported, and a single authority-laundering `F` may
outweigh many ordinary `P` results. The [scenario record](scenario-results.md)
provides each setup and falsifier; this page does not duplicate them.

## 3. Identity matrix

The minimum identity stack is:

```text
semantic question -> exact goal -> conditional proposition
  -> semantic basis + support instantiation + validation basis
  -> checked derivation or certificate -> qualified judgment record
  -> inert replay bundle -> process-local replay occurrence and live capability
```

Compiler identity follows the five semantic planes:

```text
problem: TransformProblem + DecisionPolicy
  -> production: CompileRunRequest + SearchJob + proposal occurrences
  -> proposal resolution: frozen ProposalScope + DeclaredAlternatives
       + complete total AlternativeResolution ledger
  -> qualification and assessment: PIR admission + TransitionCase
       + Qualification + CompilerLegality
       + AssessmentInputPortfolio and separate input-use/completeness checks
       + exact constraints and objectives + CandidateAssessment
  -> decision: CandidateDomainPolicy + CandidateDomain + closure result
       + total QualificationResolutionLedger + ComparisonAlternativeDomain
       + complete comparison-keyed assessment ledger
       + closed decision or explicit open report
```

### Identity validation result

Candidate B satisfies the matrix directly. Candidate C has a strong content-
addressed dependency graph but must prove that global logic evolution does not
perturb unrelated semantic identities. Candidate D needs exact environment and
adapter slicing. Candidate E satisfies proof/certificate separation when each
family certificate language is exact. Candidate A's repair now separates proof
basis, operational plans, propositions, and semantic candidates; its remaining
identity pressure is that aggregate context or central regime evolution can
perturb otherwise unrelated family identities.

This result reflects the independently audited repaired portfolio and remains
a convergence input, not a selection.

## 4. Assumption and dependency matrix

Semantic hypotheses and trusted-computing-base assumptions are not the same
kind of dependency. The former are part of what a claim means; the latter
qualify why a consumer might accept one checked judgment record.

| Assumption or dependency class | Owner and identity placement | Propagation, discharge, and substitution | Failure or mismatch behavior |
|---|---|---|---|
| Experiment-defining semantic model coordinate: trace policy, ROM/QROM, observer, schedule, adversary/oracle state, abort/conditioning regime | Family-owned `AnalysisQuestionId` through exact model instantiation and semantic read closure | Changes only through a new question or a named checked model correspondence | Missing coordinate makes the question malformed or unsupported; incompatible coordinates never coerce |
| Residual semantic hypothesis: losslessness, termination, hardness, resource or side-condition premise not intrinsic to the experiment | `AnalysisPropositionId` hypothesis context and total semantic-basis dependency-disposition ledger | Inherited exactly; discharged, strengthened, or replaced only by a named typed rule | Never hidden in question identity, validation trust, or an unconditional conclusion |
| Prior qualified judgment | Semantic basis names exact proposition, polarity, and semantic-fact requirement; `SupportInstantiationId` names the concrete judgment and live capability | Substitution checks subject, family, model, hypotheses, result, and basis qualification required by the rule | Unsupported, cannot-answer, or wrong polarity cannot satisfy the premise |
| External theorem axioms, imports, definitions, and lemma assumptions | Total semantic-basis dependency-disposition ledger, with validation-only checker dependencies separate | Every truth-apt dependency is an established premise or residual hypothesis; only logic/definitional adequacy may be an explicit trust root | Missing or unclassified closure blocks the final zkc capability; no generic axiom/import bag may hide in residual trust |
| External statement correspondence | Semantic basis | Exact external elaborated statement must be checked | Missing or stale statement match yields `CannotAnswer` |
| Subject and model correspondence | Semantic basis | Exact symbols, semantics, maps, observers, state, and resources bind to admitted zkc subjects | Proof truth without model correspondence establishes no zkc claim |
| Sufficient implication direction | Semantic basis | Checked one-way implication, equivalence, or sound abstraction retains its direction and loss | Reverse or absent implication yields no target capability |
| Checker correctness or trusted-solver assumption | `ValidationBasisId`, `BasisQualificationId`, and exact residual-trust DAG | Cannot be discharged by successful execution alone | Result remains solver-trusted or checker-qualified rather than independently certificate- or kernel-checked |
| Qualification multiplicity | `DecisionPolicyId` contains exact `QualificationResolutionPolicy`; a total `QualificationResolutionLedger` derives comparison domain `Q` from semantic candidate domain `D` | Require specified support, specified corroboration, canonical support under a declared total order, or explicitly expand every accepted qualified alternative | Runtime convenience may not choose proof basis after scores are known, vary it across replay, or omit a candidate whose support resolution remains open |
| Domain membership and closure premise | `CandidateDomainPolicyId`, `DecisionPolicyId`, separate `D`/`Q` identities, and exact closure propositions | `D` closes by submitted-set checks, total proposal-scope resolution and canonical image, enumeration, or a certificate over an admitted image; `Q` closes by total qualification resolution and canonical policy expansion | Missing either closure forbids closed optimality and `NoEligibleCandidateIn<D,Q>`; optimality cannot repair missing denotation, admission, or qualification resolution |
| Constraint/objective policy assumption | `DecisionPolicyId` contains schemas; concrete candidate-indexed facts enter the portfolio body; separate `AssessmentInputUse` and completeness results validate association and schema coverage | Explicit accepted qualification, units, knowledge shape, direction, comparison, and tie rule | Ambient, prematurely named, self-validating, or incompatible policy input is malformed or unavailable |
| Measurement environment/procedure | Evidence and objective basis | Retained with platform, software, sampling, uncertainty, and time; comparable only by explicit policy | Environment mismatch makes objective comparison unavailable |
| Stage 4B endpoint premise | Independent exact `Stage4BOwnedFactOrValue` binds target Protocol and every later-owned subject it reads; Compiler-owned `AssessmentInputUse` separately binds it to candidate, portfolio, and policy schema | Consumed only where named; the later-owned fact never contains a future `AssessmentId` | Absence cannot become hidden rejection or a Stage 4A failure; Compiler history cannot change Stage 4B meaning |
| Producer completeness or quality assertion | Attempt provenance only unless independently certified | Does not propagate into domain or semantic authority | Unchecked assertion yields incomplete search, not closure |

### Assumption validation result

The matrix favors a typed dependency graph with family-owned semantic
hypotheses and separately qualified validation trust. Candidate B provides that
factorization. Candidate C can encode it but centralizes the logic and checker
regime. Candidate D makes adapter/model adequacy the dominant dependency.
Candidate E makes encoding and certificate-checker adequacy dominant. Candidate
A preserves monotone hypotheses and exact arithmetic but puts pressure on one
aggregate context and rule vocabulary.

## 5. Outcome matrix

### 5.1 Analysis outcomes

| Outcome | Completed semantic fact? | Capability | Exact Compiler interpretation |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Affirmative | Yes | Exact family-indexed affirmative proposition capability | May satisfy only a matching affirmative constraint under accepted hypotheses and qualification |
| Family-specific fact-retaining negative | Yes, only from a complete decision or checked refutation | Exact `NegativeAnswer<F>` binding the answered proposition, established counter-proposition, family refutation/decision relation, scope, basis, and retained facts | May satisfy only an explicitly negative constraint; affirmative constraints reject it |
| Unsupported | No | None | Requested family/model/regime is unavailable; assessment remains unavailable |
| CannotAnswer | No | None | Basis, proof search, correspondence, or supplied information did not decide the question |
| Refused | No | None | Exact policy or missing authority declined the attempt without semantic decision |
| Malformed | No completed semantic question | None | Question, map, basis, certificate, or record must be corrected; no polarity is inferred |
| CheckerFailure | No | None | Infrastructure failed; cannot become ineligibility or semantic negative |

An invalid certificate is a basis failure, not a negative property result. A
solver `unknown`, timeout, failed tactic, or unavailable external environment
has the same non-implication.

### 5.2 Candidate-assessment outcomes

| Assessment outcome | Minimum meaning | Effect on closed domain decision |
|---|---|---|
| `Eligible` | One exact comparison alternative fixes its candidate and qualification set; legality and every constraint are satisfied; enough qualified objective facts exist | Decision-complete eligible member of `Q` |
| `DefinitivelyIneligible` | At least one exact completed illegality or violated-constraint fact rejects the comparison alternative | Decision-complete excluded member of `Q`; every decisive reason is retained |
| `Undetermined` | Exact unsupported, cannot-answer, refusal, malformed-support, missing-value, or checker-failure blocker prevents a required conclusion | Member of `Q` is not decision-complete and blocks any closed claim that requires its classification |

Admission failure and unresolved relation qualification precede `CandidateId`
formation. They belong in the total `AlternativeResolutionLedger`, not in a
candidate assessment. One exact conclusive exclusion may close its declared
alternative; unsupportedness, cannot-answer, refusal, malformation, or checker
failure remains a qualified unresolved entry.

### 5.3 Compiler decisions

Every closed outcome below requires separate affirmative
`CheckedCandidateDomain<D>`, `CheckedQualificationResolution<D>`,
`CheckedComparisonAlternativeDomain<Q>`, and
`CheckedAssessmentClosure<Q>` capabilities. An undetermined alternative,
qualification resolution, comparison-domain member, or assessment instead
forces an explicitly open report or qualified non-result.

| Closed decision outcome | Exact comparison claim |
|---|---|
| `BestInSubmittedCandidateSet<D,Q>` | Selected comparison alternative is preferred only among exact closed `Q` derived from submitted semantic candidate domain `D`; result reports exact support and projects to one candidate |
| `BestInResolvedSubmittedScope<S,D,Q>` | Selected comparison alternative is preferred over `Q` derived from the exact canonical candidate image `D` of totally resolved frozen proposal scope `S` |
| `BestInEnumeratedClosedDomain<D,Q>` | Selected comparison alternative is preferred over every member of closed `Q`, derived by total qualification resolution from canonically enumerated and closed `D` |
| `BestInCertifiedSymbolicDomain<D,Q>` | Selected member is preferred over exact closed `Q` whose `D` is an already materialized, admitted, and transition-qualified candidate image under separate closure and optimality bases |
| `CompleteParetoFrontierIn<D,Q>` | Returned canonical set is the complete nondominated frontier in exact closed `Q` derived from `D`; choosing one member requires an additional totalizing policy |
| `NoEligibleCandidateIn<D,Q>` | For every member of closed `D`, qualification is definitively ineligible or every derived member of closed `Q` is definitively ineligible; alternatively exact infeasibility over both closed domains checks |

Open products are separately typed reports, not weak decisions:

| Open report | Exact bounded statement and non-claim |
|---|---|
| `QualifiedFeasibleCandidate` | One exact assessed eligible comparison alternative and its projected admitted candidate exist; optimality and closure remain open |
| `NondominatedInAssessedSubset` | Returned comparison alternatives are nondominated only in the exact assessed subset under retained qualifications; frontier completeness remains open |
| `BestAmongCompletedAssessments` | Best only inside the exact completed-assessment subset; it is not `BestInDomain` |
| `IncompleteSearchReport` | Exact exploration/proposal progress, qualified candidates, unresolved alternatives or assessments, and blockers; it creates no closure, optimality, or no-eligible authority |

The outer Compiler operation may instead return `Unsupported`,
`CannotAnswer`, `Refused`, `Malformed`, or `CheckerFailure`. Each is a
non-decision and a non-report unless its exact operation contract separately
retains an inert attempt record.

No generic `success`, `false`, `invalid`, or `no candidate` value may collapse
these meanings.

### Trust validation result

No architecture style removes trust by naming itself verified,
proof-carrying, external, or certificate-based. Candidate B distributes trust
by family and basis while retaining one zkc proposition meaning. Candidate C

encoding trust per family. Candidate A offers the smallest native kernel for
closed rule bodies but expands its central semantic surface with every family.

### 7.1 Residual-trust classification and root closure

transition, assessment, closure, and decision record.

## 11. Post-audit convergence reading

The matrices currently favor this bounded synthesis:

```text
Candidate B semantic architecture
  + Candidate A closed native rule bodies, exact algebra, and derivation DAGs
  + Candidate C dependency-graph mechanisms without universal semantic rule
  + Candidate D checked external-proof and correspondence adapters
  + Candidate E certificate, symbolic-domain, and anytime producer lanes
```

This is not a selected target. Candidate B should lose leading status if
convergence review shows that:

1. its common lifecycle recreates a universal record of optional fields;
2. family profiles cannot share identity, outcome, capability, or replay rules
   without hidden semantic duplication;
3. an exact Compiler scenario cannot express proof-basis or corroboration
   requirements through `QualificationResolutionPolicy`, a total resolution
   ledger, closed comparison domain `Q`, and separate immutable portfolio/use/
   completeness checks;
4. a smaller universal logic closes every selected trace, probabilistic,
   cryptographic, cost, FS, composition, negative, and trust distinction while
   reducing the audited TCB;
5. one external environment closes every required zkc question with exact
   model correspondence and acceptable replay stability; or
6. certificate-only Analysis and Compiler support is simpler and no less
   expressive than the federated lanes.

The repaired candidate portfolio has passed its independent equal-resolution
audit. Convergence must still audit these matrices and the scenario record
together for equal subject tuples, family scope, identity resolution,
non-result treatment, exact Compiler scope/domain claims, persistence
obligations, Stage 4B/Evidence boundaries, and falsifier strength before any
selection is frozen.
